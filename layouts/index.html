About

Machine learning now enters climate modeling in two overlapping ways. Hybrid models place learned components inside process-based Earth system models. Emulators replace a single component, or in some cases the whole coupled system, with a trained network.

For climate applications both approaches must meet the same requirements. A model has to remain stable and conserve its budgets over long coupled integrations, and it has to give a physical response to forcing that lies outside the range of its training data. Current research groups approach this problem in very different ways.

The workshop brings the hybrid modeling and emulator communities together to compare their methods and to identify the main barriers. Its focus is coupled climate on time scales of years to centuries.

Themes

Hybrid ML-physics model development

Learned parameterizations and other ML components inside process-based models. Many parameterizations are already semi-empirical, and machine learning offers a better way to fit them.

Climate emulators and generalization for changing climates

Emulators of the atmosphere, the ocean and the coupled system. A central question is how they behave under forcing and climate states that are rare or absent in the training data.

Coupled hybrid and ML models and stability

Exchange of fluxes and state variables between components. Conservation and drift in long integrations are covered here.

Benchmarks, evaluation, calibration and interpretability

Shared tests and diagnostics for long climate runs, together with calibration. The theme also includes methods for finding out what a trained model has actually learned.

Format

Invited speakers will give talks of 15 to 20 minutes, one or two per theme. After every two or three talks there is a moderated discussion block of about an hour, which starts from a set question. Contributed talks and posters will be chosen from submitted abstracts.

Breaks are long, so that participants have time for informal discussion. Each breakout session will have a stated goal.

The main product of the meeting will be a peer-reviewed perspective article on the state of the field and its next steps. It will be written by the organizing committee, and participants who wish to contribute are welcome to join as co-authors.
